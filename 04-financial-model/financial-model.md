# Financial Model: Fable

> Module 5 · Master Product Financials & Strategic Bets, ★ Deliverable 4
>
> The business case for funding the strategic Rock (the memory-grounded check-in layer), and the kill criteria that would tell us to stop. Figures are stated on a discounted basis (20% cost of capital) wherever a decision turns on them, because that is the basis finance will use.

**What this bet is.** Fable is not making money on the subscribers it buys. It is roughly breaking even, and we cannot tell which side of the line we are on.

Here is the clearest way to see it. We charge $9.99 a month. Once you account for what it costs to acquire someone and how long they stay, we need **$9.57** a month just to break even. That leaves 42 cents. And that number depends on an assumption nobody has checked: that people who stay past six months keep staying at about 4% monthly churn. If the real figure is 5.2%, we need $10.20 to break even, which means **we are losing money on every subscriber, not making it.** We do not know which of those is true.

This bet brings breakeven down to **$6.40**, leaving **$3.59 of room** instead of 42 cents. That is what we are really buying: not the return, but the end of a question we cannot currently answer. The mechanism: memory-grounded check-ins raise the share of conversations rated 4+ on "felt understood" (KR2), felt-understood conversations lift 26-week retention (KR1), and 26-week retention turns a 3-month subscriber into an 11-month one. This is a Revenue-line bet that works through churn, not pricing or new acquisition.

> **The case in one paragraph:** Fable sits within cents of its cost of capital and does not know which side of it: 1.05:1 discounted on a 25-month payback at a 4% churn tail, 0.99:1 at a 5.2% tail. The memory-grounded check-in layer takes that to 1.82:1 and a 10-month discounted payback, worth about $11.6M of discounted incremental contribution a year against a ~$1.0M build, and it lowers breakeven ARPU from $9.57 to $6.40. The $3.59 of resulting headroom is the durable product here, not the return: it makes the business robust to a tail assumption nobody has measured. Three things I want to say before anyone else finds them.
>
> First, we cannot reach 3:1 with this bet. Discounted, that would need about 59% of subscribers still here at 26 weeks, five times where we are now, in a product whose users get better in four months. So 3:1 is the wrong bar for this decision. Judge it on how much room we gain above breakeven instead.
>
> Second, 74% of the value depends on the 4% tail: the assumption that people who stay past six months keep staying for about two years. Nobody has measured that, and the strategy elsewhere says these users graduate.
>
> Third, we have never measured whether feeling understood actually causes people to stay. That is why the gates below test the outcome and not just the sentiment score.

## 2. What this bet costs beyond the build

- **Opportunity cost.** The six engineers come from crisis mode and app performance, the other two Rocks. Crisis mode is the safety gate and the KR3 entry point, so this bet is partly funded by delaying the thing that licenses it.
- **Working capital.** At CAC +20% and a 14-month discounted payback, steady-state unrecovered acquisition spend is about **$7.6M** outstanding at any time. Fundable, but it is a line nobody has drawn.
- **People beyond engineering.** Memory fidelity requires a monthly clinical transcript audit and a retrieval-precision review: standing clinical advisory time, not a one-off.
- **Time to value.** First signal at week 6 on felt-understood; retention not readable for 26 weeks; discounted payback at month 10 after launch. Two quarters of exposure before the number the business cares about moves, which is why the gates below run on a leading indicator.
- **Ceiling.** Not a binding constraint here, which is what separates this from the team-tier proposal: the addressable base is the existing 4.2M-user population plus the dormant-graduate pool, not a 3,000-person segment that depletes as you convert it.
- **Inference for non-payers: the largest unpriced item.** Every figure here is per *paying* subscriber, and at 5.96 months of average life across 400,000 annual acquisitions the paying base is only about 199,000 people, roughly 5% of the 4.2M actives. KR3 explicitly targets dormant graduates having a second conversation, and those users are by definition not paying. The memory layer will therefore run for a free population an order of magnitude larger than the one it is costed against, and even modest free-side conversation volume creates a recurring cost exceeding the entire $1.0M build. This is invisible on the page precisely because everything is expressed per payer. **Sizing it is the second diligence item after CAC composition.**
- **Net, not upside, on the existing base.** The $0.30 COGS increase lands on the whole paying base including the ~199,000 subscribers for whom no retention benefit is modelled: roughly $0.7M a year of certain cost against uncertain benefit. Dormant-graduate reactivation (KR3) at ~$2 against $38 CAC is genuine upside excluded from the $11.6M, but the two should be described as a net position rather than as pure headroom.

### Which P&L lines this moves

Forward, from the decision: memory-grounded check-ins raise felt-understood, which lifts 26-week retention, which shows up first on **Revenue** as reduced churn, then extends LTV and shortens CAC payback. The build itself lands on **Operating Expenses** (~$1.0M), and the memory layer authors **COGS** directly, adding ~$0.30 per subscriber-month of inference and pulling **gross margin** from about 83% to 80%. Net effect on **Operating Income**: about $11.6M of discounted annual contribution against roughly $1.0M of build and a modest ongoing clinical-audit line.

Backward, the harder direction, and the reason the gates below are instrumented the way they are: if **gross margin compresses** next quarter, the first place to look is memory depth per conversation, not pricing. If **CAC payback lengthens**, look at early retention and activation before blaming acquisition spend. If **NRR or revenue softens while felt-understood holds**, the transfer coefficient is the suspect, which is exactly what kill criterion 3 exists to detect before the P&L shows it.

## 3. Kill criteria

Seven gates. Build timeline assumed: 1 July to 31 December 2026, $1.0M total ($750K engineering over two quarters, $250K clinical audit and holdout instrumentation), spending roughly $500K per quarter.

> 1. **Interim read.** If **felt-understood 4+ share** has not reached **47%** by **30 September 2026**, we **stop the memory workstream and redeploy all six engineers to crisis mode for Q4**, releasing the ~$500K of build budget not yet spent. Redeploying rather than holding matters: a gate that pauses the work without moving the people keeps the burn running while the option is held.
> 2. **Primary gate.** If **felt-understood 4+ share** has not reached **55%** by **31 December 2026**, we **do not commit the Q1 2027 headcount**: the six engineers (about $375K per quarter, $1.5M annualized) return to crisis mode and performance before the Q1 plan locks. By this date the $1.0M build is spent, so the reversal is forward run rate, not remaining budget.
> 3. **Thesis gate.** Full delivery requires a **20-point lift in 12-week retention** (34.6% to 54.8%). Below a **12-point lift**, we **stop and return the team**, because 12 points yields about $6.2M against $11.6M. Between 12 and 20 points the bet **continues at reduced scope with the difference reallocated**. Read date is **31 January 2027**, not December: holdout enrolment **closes** 1 October 2026 (~1,000 per arm), and a 12-week read on the last enrolled subscriber cannot mature by 31 December unless the entire cohort enrols on a single day. This gate binds even if felt-understood clears 55%. *Gate 3 governs where it and gate 2 disagree: gate 2 measures a leading indicator, gate 3 measures the outcome. At a transfer coefficient of 0.47 a bare pass of gate 2 implies only a 10.1-point lift and would be caught here, which is the intended behaviour.*
> 4. **Cost gate, calibrated to the transition window rather than to success.** COGS rises the day the memory layer ships; retention does not respond for at least 26 weeks. Breakeven COGS **at today's retention is $2.12**, not the $5.59 that applies once 30% is delivered. So: if **fully loaded COGS** exceeds **$2.60 per subscriber-month** at any monthly reading before the KR1 result is in, we **cap memory depth at the last compliant configuration and re-forecast within 30 days**. The looser $3.50 threshold applies only after 26-week retention is confirmed above 25%.
> 5. **Price gate, moved earlier.** If the pricing pilot returns realized blended ARPU below **$9.60** by **30 November 2026**, before the build completes and before the next year's acquisition budget is committed, we **halt scaling and re-underwrite**. $9.60 is set against the **pre-success** breakeven of $9.57, not the $6.40 the bet eventually buys: until retention actually moves, anything under $9.57 means we are acquiring subscribers below cost.
> 6. **Tail gate, the one covering 74% of the value.** Read **months 7 through 12 retention** on the first memory-layer cohort by **30 June 2027** against the assumed 4% monthly tail. Above a realized **5.5%**, we **freeze acquisition volume growth and re-underwrite before committing the following year's CAC budget**, since at 5.2% the baseline business already misses its cost of capital. Nothing else in this document tests the assumption that carries most of the money.
> 7. **CAC gate.** If confirming CAC composition (the first diligence item) returns a fully loaded figure above **$45**, we **cut acquisition volume before scaling the bet**, because at $45 the baseline ratio is 0.89:1 and when you are losing money per subscriber the right move is to buy fewer of them, not to build faster.
